The Kobe N-of-1 Pathway

From your own tumor data to a reviewed treatment made from your own cells, in seven steps. One owner for each step, so you always know who to call.

Steps 0 and 1 happen at home. Steps 2 to 4 run in parallel. Step 5 is the only time you must be in Kobe. A Class III case takes about 4 to 6 months in total.

Seven steps, one owner for each.

  1. 0

    Intake and eligibility · at home · 1–2 weeks

    A video call and your records. A doctor who gets no money from your choice asks three questions. Does your disease fit? Is your body strong enough? Is a clinical trial better for you?

    Who: CellHaus intake, with a partner hospital’s international patient office.

  2. 1

    Data and design · at home · 2–4 weeks

    Get your tumor sample, raw data files and immune type (HLA). Find the targets in your tumor. Use your own tools or ours.

    Who: Gene tests in Kobe, open tools for target design, and a letter from your home oncologist.

  3. 2

    Sponsor and site · in parallel · 2–6 weeks

    A licensed Kobe doctor takes legal responsibility for your treatment.

    Who: A partner academic hospital in Kobe.

  4. 3

    Manufacturing plan · in parallel · 2–4 weeks

    A licensed cell facility agrees to make one batch for one person.

    Who: A partner licensed cell facility in Kobe.

  5. 4

    Committee review and filing · in parallel · 1–6 months

    A certified committee reviews the plan. The doctor files it with the regulator.

    Who: Unchain Bio drafts the plan. A Kobe partner books a free meeting with the national regulator.

  6. 5

    Treatment in Kobe · in Kobe · 4–10 weeks

    The team collects your blood on day one. The lab makes your cells in 1 to 3 weeks. You get doses over the next weeks. You live at CellHaus, near the hospitals on Port Island.

    Who: Your sponsor hospital, plus CellHaus housing, an interpreter and a care guide.

  7. 6

    Follow-up and open registry · at home · 12+ months

    Your home doctor takes over scans and blood tests. Results go into an open registry, good or bad, so the next patient starts further ahead.

    Who: Your home oncologist. The sponsor doctor files reports. CellHaus publishes with your consent.

All times are planning estimates.

Three review paths

The risk class sets the clock.

Class III

Lowest risk

Your own immune cells, changed very little. Example: dendritic cell vaccines.

Certified committee reviews the plan. Then file with the Kinki regional bureau. Then start.

About 2–4 months

Class II

Middle risk

Cells grown in the lab to large numbers. Example: TIL from your tumor.

Special certified committee reviews the plan. Then file with the ministry. Then start.

About 4–6 months

Class I

Highest risk

Gene-edited cells, CAR-T, iPS-derived cells and in vivo gene therapy.

Special certified committee, then a ministry review and a waiting period, then start.

About 6–12 months

Times are planning estimates, from plan filing to first dose. A certified committee and counsel confirm the class for each case.

The family track

Parents build companies for rare disease. Kobe can give them a first patient.

The model works. Parents of children with Fanconi anemia, a skin blister disease and Duchenne helped start the programs behind Rocket, Abeona’s Zevaskyn and Capricor.

The gap. A US priority review voucher can be worth about $200M, but only after FDA approval. That takes years.

The Kobe role. For a gene-corrected cell or gene therapy, Kobe can give a reviewed, regulated first case in parallel with the US path. Class I, so expect 6 to 12 months.
